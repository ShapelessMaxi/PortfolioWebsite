<h1>Self-perception vs external perception</h1>
<p>I've already explored this theme, but I feel like there is more to uncover or just more creative inspiration lying there. In Glitch Armor, I speak of the anxiety induced by being perceived that's heightened when you are visibly queer. It's our instinct to dress and present in a queer fashion, and it might inspire people to give compliments or present in an authentic way themselves, but it also might provoke other people's homo/transphobia. In To Dissect / To Stitch, I dove into the societal perception and stereotypes that comes with having a modified and tattooed body, and I wrote about how it can be freeing to disconnect yourself from the way societal norms shape your physical and emotional self.</p>
<p>What if there were two digital characters looking at themselves face-to-face through the screens and just being, while at the same time looking at themselves through webcams. That webcam feed could be analyzed and modified, and then displayed on screens that were only visible to the viewers, aka facing backwards (<- screen of consciousness / right character -> webcam -> <- webcam <- left character / screen of consciousness ->). *btw is Screen of Consciousness a genius title???</p>
<p>This setup would evoke how we can never know what other people think. It would show how our presentation can be seen in a completely different way.</p>

<h1>TouchDesigner Prototype 02 - November 2024</h1>
<p>Scratch this pile of operators, I need a script that will do all the simple operations of this colorAnalyzeTOPs in a for loop. Also, there's probably a way to create operators and so I'll be able to change, modify, expand it easily ans faster.</p>
<p>I'm starting to work with NumPy because basically, you can read an image as a really long 2D array populated by every pixel of the image. The pixels are an object. There's useful methods like split(), making it possible to digest the array by smaller areas, and variables, like the .r, .g and .b values of each pixels. In brief, the NumPy array and split into cells[x,y]. The RGB values of each pixels of each cells are averaged, basically smudging them all together. I export both an image, and a table containing all colors in written form (this was more for debugging purposes). Then, to create the visual, I am storing a 3Dspheres node system in a baseComp, and copying it a bunch as much times as the image is split. The colors of each sphere changes according to what's in front of the camera, and to what colors are in the reference Table. They get smaller if the input's color is not registered. This process is dynamic and live and not slow!</p>

<h2>In this prototype, I'm doing a few very sensible things</h2>
<p>- storing a dynamic reference color table</p>
<p>- storing x and y size of the split/grid</p>
